What If There Were No IT Sector After Three Years?

Imagine a world without software companies, developers, cloud platforms, digital networks, cybersecurity teams or information systems. This is not a forecast; it is a thought experiment showing how deeply technology supports modern life.

What does “no IT” mean?

Here, “no IT” means the disappearance or severe failure of the digital systems operating banking, hospitals, transport, education, government, manufacturing, communication and commerce. Electricity and mechanical tools might remain, but the digital coordination layer would be missing.

Reality check: This is an extreme scenario. Removing IT would not only remove programming jobs; it would interrupt systems used by almost every modern institution.

Problems the world would face

Banking and payments

Digital banking, ATMs, card payments, online transfers, fraud detection and financial records would be severely disrupted. Cash and paper records would become more important.

Healthcare

Patient records, appointments, medical imaging, laboratory systems, emergency coordination and connected devices would become slower and more error-prone.

Transport and logistics

Air traffic, railway scheduling, navigation, shipping, warehouses and delivery networks would face major failures.

Education and research

Online classes, digital libraries, research databases and collaboration tools would disappear, increasing geographical inequality.

Government services

Digital identity, tax filing, welfare databases, land records and public health monitoring would be slower and harder to manage.

Businesses and jobs

Companies would lose customer databases, online sales, payroll systems, analytics and remote collaboration, affecting many non-IT workers too.

How would India be affected?

India would face serious disruption because digital payments, online government portals, digital education, telemedicine, e-commerce, IT exports and Global Capability Centres depend on technology infrastructure.

A sudden disappearance of IT could affect service exports, tax collection, financial inclusion, startups, employment, public administration and international competitiveness. Small businesses using digital payments and online marketplaces could lose customers and cash flow.

Without developers, who maintains systems?

Software does not maintain itself. Developers, system administrators, database specialists, network engineers, security analysts and technical support teams build, monitor, update and repair digital systems. Without them, vulnerabilities would remain unpatched and failures would spread across connected services.

Could society return to manual systems?

Some activities could return to paper, cash and face-to-face communication. However, manual alternatives would not match the scale, speed, searchability, backup and coordination of modern digital systems.

The deeper lesson: IT is not only about computers. It is the coordination system behind modern society.

Conclusion

A world without IT would mean slower communication, weaker coordination, reduced access to services, economic disruption and greater inequality. The better question is how to make technology safer, more inclusive, sustainable and resilient.
