What Could the IT Sector Look Like After Three Years?

By 2029, the IT sector is unlikely to disappear. It is more likely to become AI-assisted, automated, security-focused and deeply connected to every industry.

The central change: from writing code to designing intelligent systems

Generative AI can already help with code generation, testing, documentation, debugging and repetitive maintenance. As these tasks become faster, system design, product thinking, security, data quality and human judgment become more valuable.

Expert signal: The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 estimates that 22% of jobs could be disrupted by 2030, while 170 million roles may be created and 92 million displaced. This points to transformation and reskilling—not the disappearance of all work.

Major changes expected by 2029

01

AI-native development

Developers will increasingly use coding assistants, agents, automated testing and AI-based code review. Human oversight will remain necessary for reliability and accountability.

02

Cloud and edge computing

Cloud platforms and edge systems will support factories, healthcare devices, logistics, connected vehicles and real-time services.

03

Cybersecurity

Identity protection, secure software supply chains, privacy engineering and incident response will become essential business functions.

04

AI governance

Companies will need accurate data, transparent processes, privacy controls and accountability for AI decisions.

05

High-value technology work

Demand may grow for AI integration, cloud modernization, cybersecurity, product engineering and industry-specific software.

06

Continuous learning

Practical portfolios, AI literacy, communication and problem-solving will become increasingly important alongside degrees.

What does growth mean for India?

India has a strong base in software services, engineering talent, global delivery and digital infrastructure. Nasscom's FY2026 outlook, reported by Reuters, projected India's technology sector to reach about $315 billion, with 6.1% year-on-year growth and approximately 135,000 net new jobs.

India's next stage may depend on moving beyond labour-intensive service delivery toward AI products, research, cybersecurity, cloud platforms, digital public infrastructure and industry-focused innovation.

Important: Industry revenue growth does not mean every worker benefits equally. Routine coding, support and repetitive outsourcing may face pressure, while architecture, security, domain knowledge and leadership may become more valuable.

The process of change

  1. Experimentation with AI tools.
  2. Integration into development and business workflows.
  3. Redesign of teams and processes.
  4. Governance for privacy, security and reliability.
  5. Specialisation in AI engineering, evaluation, data governance and human-AI collaboration.

Conclusion

By 2029, the IT sector may be smaller in some routine activities but greater in strategic importance. The strongest professionals will understand users, systems, data, security and responsible AI—not merely code syntax.
