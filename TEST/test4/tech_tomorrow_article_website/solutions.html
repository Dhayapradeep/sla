If We Could Change the Future, What Solution Would We Choose?

The answer is not to stop technology. It is to build a future where AI and IT increase human capability, protect people, create opportunity and remain accountable.

The goal: human-centred technological progress

If technology changes the world, society must change how it prepares people and manages risk. The solution should combine innovation with education, strong institutions, ethical design, cybersecurity and access for everyone.

Seven practical solutions

1. Teach AI literacy

Schools, colleges and workplaces should teach people how AI works, how to verify outputs, protect data and use AI responsibly.

2. Reskill workers

Provide affordable training in cloud, cybersecurity, data, communication, product thinking, AI tools and domain-specific skills.

3. Build responsible AI

AI products need testing, human review, privacy protection, bias evaluation, audit trails and clear accountability.

4. Protect infrastructure

Critical services need backups, disaster recovery, secure software supply chains, identity controls and incident-response plans.

5. Strengthen India's ecosystem

India can invest in research, domestic compute, semiconductor capabilities, cybersecurity, public-interest technology and trusted AI products.

6. Solve real social problems

Developers can build for healthcare, agriculture, education, accessibility, climate resilience, transport and small businesses.

7. Measure more than profit

Technology should be judged by safety, inclusion, environmental impact, reliability, job quality and public value.

A possible action plan

  1. Students: Learn programming, databases, communication, AI literacy and problem-solving; build useful projects.
  2. Developers: Use AI assistants while strengthening system design, testing, security and debugging.
  3. Companies: Introduce AI with training, transparent evaluation and worker participation.
  4. Colleges: Combine theory with projects, internships, ethics and interdisciplinary learning.
  5. Governments: Support connectivity, digital inclusion, privacy, cybersecurity, research and worker transition.

What experts suggest

The World Economic Forum highlights upskilling and reskilling as technology changes jobs. Its India-focused analysis also notes that employers are seeking broader talent pools and investing in training and alternative pathways.

McKinsey's research suggests that generative AI can increase developer productivity, but value requires redesigned workflows, quality controls, leadership and responsible implementation.

Research on AI-augmented developers points toward a combined skill set: generative AI use, core software engineering, adjacent technical knowledge and communication.

Our principle: “Automate tasks, not human dignity. Improve productivity, but invest in people. Build powerful systems, but keep humans accountable.”

Conclusion

The future of IT should not be measured only by jobs lost or lines of code generated. It should be measured by whether technology helps people live safer, healthier, more productive and more meaningful lives.

If India prepares early, it can move from being mainly a technology-services destination to becoming a creator of trusted AI products, resilient infrastructure and inclusive digital solutions.
